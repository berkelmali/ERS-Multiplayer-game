About This Game

🃏 What this is

Egyptian Rat Screw is a reflex card game: cards go down one at a time, and when the pile makes a pattern, the first hand to slap it takes the whole stack. This version runs in a browser with no download — one click starts a match against three bots. You only need an account if you want your scores on the leaderboard or want to play against real people.

🛠️ How it is built

Plain JavaScript modules, no framework and no build step: the files your browser downloads are the files that were written. Firebase handles sign-in, the leaderboard and multiplayer rooms. The slap patterns live in one registry that both the browser and the server read, so the two can never disagree about what counts as a slap. A release ships only after the rule engine, the scoring maths and a scripted walkthrough in a real browser all pass.

⚖️ How it stays fair

In multiplayer a contested pile does not go to whoever's message reaches the server first. It goes to the lower reaction time, measured from the moment the card landed on the screen, so a faster connection is not an advantage. Joining a table is checked on the server, never in the page. And the Daily Challenge deals every player in the world the same position from the same seed, with your personal settings overridden while it is scored — otherwise an easier setting would buy a higher score on the same board.

👤 Who made it

Built and maintained by Berk Elmalı as an independent project. There is no publisher behind it and no team — feature requests, bug reports and disagreements about the rules all arrive in the same inbox.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 29 August 2026

👤 Account

Signing in uses Firebase Authentication. We store the account id it gives us and the display name you choose. If you sign in with email, that email is held by Firebase Authentication. You can play offline against bots without an account at all.

🎮 Game data

Your username and scores are stored in Firebase Firestore so leaderboards can exist. Multiplayer matches are coordinated through Firebase Realtime Database and the room data is deleted when the match ends. A Daily Challenge result is written with exactly nine fields: your account id, your username, the score, your reaction time, whether you finished the run, how long it took, the scenario profile you were given, your starting hand size, and the time you submitted it — nothing else.

💾 Stored in your browser

Settings, house rules, slap statistics, your Daily Challenge result for the day, your daily wheel spin, coins and card skins are kept in your browser's localStorage. They never leave your device unless you are signed in and a score is submitted. Clearing your browser data removes all of it.

Game Rules

🏆 How to Win

Collect all 52 cards to win the game. Cards are added to the bottom of your deck when you win the pile.

💀 How to Lose

When you play or burn your last card and cannot slap back in, you are eliminated. You can still slap the pile even with 0 cards — a successful slap returns cards to your hand!

🃏 Gameplay

Players take turns clockwise playing the top card of their deck face-up onto the center pile. The goal is to collect all cards via valid slaps or won challenges.

⚔️ Face Card Challenges (J / Q / K / A)

Playing a face card forces the next player to respond with their own face card:

  • Jack (J) — 1 chance to respond
  • Queen (Q) — 2 chances to respond
  • King (K) — 3 chances to respond
  • Ace (A) — 4 chances to respond

If the defender plays all their chances without a face card, the attacker wins the entire pile. If the defender plays a face card, the challenge reverses and the attacker must now defend.

👋 Slap Rules

Any player can slap the pile at any time. Slapping on a valid pattern wins the pile. Slapping on an invalid pattern costs you a burn card (penalty).

  • 🟡 Doubles — Two cards of the same rank in a row. e.g. 7 → 7

    7♣
  • 🟠 Tens — Two number cards that add up to 10. e.g. 3 → 7

    7♠
  • 💍 Marriage — King and Queen next to each other. e.g. K → Q
    K♣
    Q♥
  • 🥪 Sandwich — Two cards of the same rank with exactly one card between them. e.g. 8 → 5 → 8

🔥 Burn Cards

An invalid slap forces you to burn a card from your hand — it goes to a separate penalty pool. When anyone wins the pile, burn-pile cards are awarded first, followed by the played cards in chronological order, all added to the bottom of the winner's deck.

⏱️ AFK / Idle Timeout

If a player fails to play a card within the allowed time (Easy: 20s, Medium: 15s, Hard: 10s), they automatically burn a card and the turn passes to the next player.

⚡ Reflex Calibration Tool

Test and benchmark your average response reaction speed in the Settings menu! Compare your reflexes directly against simulated Challenger Bot speeds to calibrate your game.

🎯 Perfect Slap precision

Slapping very close to the center of the pile (within 15 pixels) triggers a Perfect Slap bonus! Triggers special neon star particles and custom sound chime multipliers.

🛡️ Combustion Shield

Achieving a 3-slap streak (slaps only) activates a fiery Combustion Shield valid for 30 seconds! Performing another successful slap within 30s renews the shield timer. The shield protects your deck from burning a card once per activation by shattering.

🔄 Reconnection & Bot Takeover

If your connection drops in multiplayer, an automated Bot temporarily takes over your slot so the match continues. You have a grace period to reconnect and seamlessly resume control of your deck and active streaks!

👁️ Spectator Mode & Slap Back

Eliminated? Don't leave yet! You enter Spectator Mode where you can watch the match, send quick chat emojis, and still attempt to Slap Back In at any time. A successful slap resurrects you with the pile!

⚡ Reflex Speed & HUD Badges

Every successful slap displays your response reaction speed in milliseconds in the action log. Slapping under 400ms grants a Fast Slap bonus and shows dynamic speed badges (⚡ GODSPEED, ☄️ LIGHTNING, etc.) in the HUD!

📈 Match History & SVG Analytics

Check out your Account Profile to view your complete match history, card win records, and an interactive SVG-rendered reflex speed trend curve showing your reaction improvement over time.

📜 Where the game comes from

Egyptian Rat Screw is a slapping variant of Beggar-My-Neighbour, an old English children's game in which players lay cards down in turn and a face card forces the next player to pay a penalty. The slapping half comes from the Slapjack family. The combination travels under a long list of names — ERS, ERF, Egyptian War, Bloodystump, Ratslap — and there is no documented account of where the "Egyptian" came from. The game spread by being taught at kitchen tables, not by being published, so nobody wrote that part down.

🏠 Why everyone's rules are different

Because it spread by word of mouth, almost every group plays a slightly different set. Doubles and sandwiches are close to universal; tens, marriages, top-bottom and four-in-a-row are the ones people argue about. Rather than pick a side, this game makes them switches: the four classic patterns are on by default, three more stay off until you turn them on, and the table you are about to sit at shows which set is live before the first card is dealt — so nobody discovers a house rule by losing a pile to it.

🎯 What actually makes you better

A wrong slap costs a card off the bottom of your deck, so the cheapest improvement is not slapping faster — it is slapping less often on patterns you are unsure of. Tens are what people miss: the two cards must add to exactly ten and face cards do not count, which is easy to see one beat too late. Watch the pile grow rather than the card being played; a five-card pile is worth several turns of risk, a two-card pile is not. And while a slap streak is shielding you, a wrong slap breaks the shield instead of burning a card — so on a streak, a marginal slap is worth more than it usually is.

🗓️ Why the Daily Challenge is the same for everyone

The day's deal is generated from the date itself, not from a roll on the server, so every player in the world starts from the same mid-match position with the same cards in the same hands. The scenario shape and the bot difficulty are functions of the date too. While a scored run is going your own settings are overridden — the turn clock, the animation speed and the rule set are the day's, not yours. Otherwise a gentler setting would buy a higher score on an identical board, and the leaderboard would rank preferences instead of reflexes.

🎭 Bot Personalities

Each seat plays differently: Blitz is fast and bluffs often, Chaos is wildly unpredictable, and Viper is patient and rarely wrong. Same difficulty, three different fights.
